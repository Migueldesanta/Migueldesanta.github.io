## Scope

A coverage report on European and UK sovereign bond markets prepared as part of the macro research workflow at Nittan Securities Asia. The note spans four interrelated themes:

1. **Central-bank policy transmission** — how the ECB and BoE policy stances differ in their pass-through to peripheral and core sovereign curves.
2. **Sovereign credit dynamics** — France, Italy, and Spain, with attention to where policy-driven and idiosyncratic credit components separate.
3. **UK Gilt pressures** — the political-premium component, structural buyer base shifts, and the **LDI channel** as a source of forced selling and convexity demand.
4. **US Treasury spillover** — the contribution of UST yield moves to European long-end yields, decomposed by tenor and regime.

## Method

The empirical work is built around a **cross-country event-study framework** designed to detect the price impact of *low-attention political signals* — events material to sovereign risk but not necessarily front-page news. The framework controls for:

- Concurrent macroeconomic data releases
- Cross-market spillovers (US Treasuries, German Bunds as comparison benchmarks)
- Local-currency vs. EUR-denominated effects
- Tenor-structure of the response (short vs. long end)

## The headline finding

Within this framework, **the political-premium component of UK Gilt spreads is identified at approximately 80% of the total excess over German Bunds**. The remaining ~20% is attributable to fundamental credit and rate-expectation differentials.

The decomposition matters because the political component is not arbitraged by the same mechanisms that price the fundamental component — it responds to a different information set, on a different horizon, with different liquidity. For position sizing and risk decomposition, treating it as one homogeneous spread is wrong.

## The LDI angle

The 2022 UK LDI crisis remains the most consequential episode for understanding modern Gilt-market microstructure. This note treats LDI not as a closed historical event but as an ongoing structural feature of the buyer base — affecting the convexity of long-end demand, the speed of forced-selling cascades, and the central bank's reaction function. The framework explicitly models LDI-related fragility as a state variable rather than a one-off shock.
