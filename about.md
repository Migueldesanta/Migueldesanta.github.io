I am a quantitative researcher trained at Penn State University in **Statistics** and **Political Science**. My work brings rigorous causal-inference methods — difference-in-differences, threshold regression, instrumental variables, event studies — to questions in **fixed income, monetary policy, and computational social science**.

I am currently with **Nittan Securities Asia Limited** in Hong Kong, where I produce sovereign-bond and Federal Reserve policy research, and build the dashboards that turn the research into something a desk can actually use.

## Background

I graduated from **The Pennsylvania State University** in May 2025 with a B.S. in Statistics and Political Science (GPA 3.59 / 4.0). My coursework concentrated on the methodological side — probability theory, mathematical statistics, stochastic modeling, time-series analysis, nonparametric statistics, applied machine learning — alongside the substantive side from political science (American state policy, public opinion, political economy of China). I spent a semester at **The Chinese University of Hong Kong** on exchange.

Before Nittan I led finance, risk, and strategic planning at **Aurea Media Limited**, an early-stage overseas short-drama distribution startup based between Chengdu and Hong Kong. Earlier I worked on overseas operations at **TAL Education Group** and on survey research at the **Penn State Social Science Research Institute**.

## Research approach

I am interested in problems where the methodological work and the substantive question are genuinely entangled — where the answer depends on what you assume the identifying variation is, and where the credibility of the answer is bounded by the credibility of the design.

In practice this means I spend a lot of time on identification: thinking about what plausible threats to a causal claim look like, what the equivalent of a placebo test would be, what a sceptical reviewer would attack first. When I publish a result I try to publish the limitations alongside it. The footer of the Fed dashboard surfacing β₂'s failure to converge to the LVJ benchmark is not an aesthetic flourish — it's the honest version of the model.
